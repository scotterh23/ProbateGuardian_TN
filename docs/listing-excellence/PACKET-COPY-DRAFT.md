## Page 5. The house and the court

**PG**

Two different jobs.

Your attorney handles the estate. Filings, the court, who is allowed to sign, and what the will or the heirs require. We do not give that advice. We do not file at court. We are not paid by the law firm.

We handle the property. The house, the numbers, the sale.

**Before we list**

The court names a person to act by issuing letters. Families often call that "letters of authority." Until your attorney tells us the right person can sign, we will not put the house on the market.

We can still do the useful early work: see the house, secure it, take photos for you, and prepare both numbers. That way you are ready when your attorney says the estate can move.

Sometimes the personal representative signs. Sometimes the heirs sign too. Sometimes the court has to approve the sale. Your attorney tells us which. We follow that. We do not guess.

If you do not have an attorney yet, say so on [MEETING DAY]. Most estates need one. We can point you to more than one probate attorney. You choose.

## Page 6. The vacant house

**PG. If the house is occupied, Carrie swaps the headline to "While the house is empty later" or drops the page if they will live there until closing. Default for estate files is to keep it.**

An empty house still costs money and still needs a person.

**Someone has to watch**

- Who has keys, and who is allowed in
- Whether the power, water, and heat stay on
- The lawn, the mail, and the obvious leaks
- A neighbor calling because a door is open

**What it costs every month it sits**

Taxes, insurance, utilities, and basic upkeep do not pause because the house is empty. That is one reason we show you a faster cash number and a market number side by side. Speed has a value. So does waiting for the market. You should see both before you choose.

We will not print a made-up monthly dollar figure. If you have the tax bill, the insurance bill, or the utility average, bring them. We will use your numbers.

**Insurance**

If no one lives in the house, call your insurance agent and tell them. Vacant houses are often treated differently. We are not insurance agents. We cannot tell you if you are covered. We can help you make a simple list of what is going on at the house so you can send it to them and to your attorney.

## Page 10. Two paths

**CORE**

Same team either way. We put both numbers in front of you. We are not paid more if you take cash. We are not the buyer.

**As-is cash**

We show you offers from independent cash buyers we work with. This path is for when the house is a burden and you want fewer showings and a faster finish. It is often a lower number. You will see that number, not a slogan.

You can say no to every cash offer.

**Open market**

We list the house when you can give the market a real chance. Photos, the local listing service (the MLS), showings, and negotiation. This is how you find out what a buyer will pay with time on your side. Our listing commission is paid at closing, the same as any normal listing.

**Read this before you pick**

Cash is not always better. An empty house costs money every month it sits. A market listing is not always better either, if the roof is tired and the family cannot wait. That is why both numbers sit on one page you can forward.

We do not write the final numbers into this packet. We write them after we walk the house. If a cash buyer has not answered by [MEETING DAY], we will tell you that, and we will still show you the market number.

## Page 11. How we look at price

**CORE**

We do not price the house from a website estimate.

We look at three groups:

- Similar homes that sold recently
- Homes for sale now, because that is the competition
- Homes already under contract, when we can see them

We adjust for size, updates, and condition. A renovated house down the street is not the same as a house that still needs a clean-out. We will say that out loud.

**The number**

The price we recommend is part of the meeting, after the walk. It is not hidden in this packet, and it is not skipped. You will hear a range and the reason.

## Page 14. How we get paid

**CORE**

Nothing up front. No retainer for the first visit, the photos, or the two sets of numbers.

We are paid a listing commission at closing, from the sale. The amount is agreed in writing before you sign. It is not set by law. It is fully negotiable. We will put the number on the page. We will not make you hunt for it.

[AGREED RATE: leave blank in the template. Scott writes it on the live file or says it at the table. Do not invent a percent here.]

We are not paid more if you take a cash offer. We are not the buyer.

We do not pay or receive referral fees from attorneys.

**If you choose to pay the buyer's agent**

Buyers have their own agents, and those agents sign their own agreement with the buyer before they tour. Sometimes a seller agrees to pay some or all of that cost, because it can widen who will come. Sometimes a seller does not.

If you want to offer it, we write the amount down first. We do not hide it in the listing. We do not bury it in the private notes agents see. You can decide at the offer, house by house, instead of guessing today.

**What else comes out of the check**

At the offer, we show a one-page net: price, our commission, any buyer-agent amount you agreed, and the closing costs we know. Items we do not know yet are labeled unknown. We do not invent a title bill to make the page look finished.
