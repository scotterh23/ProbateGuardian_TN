# Presentation script

What Scott or Branton says at the appointment, in order. Speaker notes for the Gamma deck. Not a memo to read in a monotone. If a line feels fake in your mouth, cut it before the meeting.

**Length:** about 60 to 75 minutes with the walk. The seated part is about 25 to 35 minutes.

**Order is the point.** Walk first. Their goal second. Price before marketing. Pay before any paperwork. No "sign tonight."

**Tags:** CORE every time. PG on estate appointments. On a normal Hardesty listing, skip every PG block and do not apologize for the shorter meeting.

**Numbers:** say only figures from this file's MLS pull and from real cash conversations. If you do not have a number, say you do not have it yet. The script uses blanks on purpose.

---

## Before you ring the bell

**CORE, in the car**

- Packet sent, or a print copy in hand
- Preliminary homes reviewed. You can point to three without opening a novel
- You know who else decides, and whether an attorney is in the file
- Phone on do not disturb once you are inside, except the house line if Shelly is covering a live emergency

**PG, in the car**

- Letter status: issued, pending, or unknown
- Do not bring a listing contract out of the bag until the signer question is answered
- If the family is in the first raw week, today's win is a secured house and a clear next step, not a signature

---

## 1. Door and walk

**Slides:** none yet. Phone or paper notes. Slide 3 gets their words after.

**CORE**

"Thanks for having us. I want to walk it with you before we sit down. I'll save the plan for the table. While we walk, tell me what you already know is wrong, and what you hope a buyer never notices."

Walk every room a buyer will walk. Attic and garage if they matter. Look at the roof from the yard, the panel, the water heater, stains, smells, pets, clutter, what would have to be gone before photos.

Write, do not perform. You are not a home inspector. If you see something dangerous (active leak, gas, break-in), say so plainly and suggest they call the right trade or the insurer. Do not diagnose the policy.

**Questions while you walk**

- "What would you want finished before anyone else sees it?"
- "What are you willing to spend, and what do you want to leave alone?"
- "Is anyone still living here? Any dates I should know?"

**PG, during the walk, only if it fits the room**

- "Has the family already pulled out what they want to keep?"
- "Anything in here that is not going with the house? Cars, tools, a safe?"
- "Who else has a key?"

Do not narrate their grief. One line is enough if you need it: "We'll go at your pace." Then be useful.

**End of the walk**

"Let's sit. I've got a short deck so we stay on track. You can stop me."

---

## 2. Open the deck

**Slide 1. Title**

"[FIRST NAME], this is for [SHORT ADDRESS], today. It's the same packet you have, just the parts we need to decide."

**Slide 2. Today, in order**

"Four things. What done looks like for you. What the house is worth in this condition, and what changes if we clean it up. How we'd take it to market if you want that. And how we get paid. Then you tell me the next step. You do not have to decide in this chair."

---

## 3. What done looks like

**Slide 3. Mostly blank. Read back what you heard.**

"Here's what I heard on the walk. Tell me if I have it wrong."

Then shut up. Write their correction on the slide or the page.

**Prompts if they are vague**

- "Is the goal a date, a dollar, or just having it over?"
- "Who do you have to explain this to?"
- "If we could only solve one problem, which one is it: time, money, or the hassle of the house?"

**PG prompt**

- "What would feel fair to the person who isn't here?"

This answer drives the recommendation. If they say "over by the end of the month," you do not spend twenty minutes on staging theory. If they say "the most the market will pay, and we can wait," you do not lead with cash.

---

## 4. Team

**Slide 4. One minute. Do not read biographies.**

"You'll deal with three people. I'm on the sale. Branton is the other set of eyes at the house. Shelly answers during the day so you're not chasing a voicemail. Same number on the packet. We are a small crew on purpose."

If Branton is in the room, he says one sentence on what he will actually do at this house, then stops.

---

## 5. House and court

**Slide 5. PG**

"Your attorney does the law. We do the house. We don't give legal advice, we don't file at court, and we don't take a fee from the law firm. If those two jobs stay separate, this goes smoother."

"We can secure the house and build the numbers before the court paperwork is finished. We don't list until your attorney says the right person can sign."

---

## 6. Fluency questions

**Slide 6. PG. This is a conversation. Check off, do not interrogate.**

"A few questions before I recommend a path. Unknown is a fine answer."

1. "Who does your attorney say can sign a listing and a deed? Are letters issued, or not yet?"
2. "Is there a co-executor or other heirs who have to agree? How do they want to get the same update you get?"
3. "Is the house vacant? Have you told the insurance company that no one lives here? I'm not the one to tell you if you're covered. Your agent is."
4. "Who has keys? Are the utilities on? Any leak or alarm I should know this week?"
5. "Has anyone walked through for keepsakes?"
6. "Any cars or other property that have to be sold or moved before we can photograph?"
7. "Is anyone living here, even short term?"
8. "Mortgage, HOA, or a bill you've already found? We'll confirm with title. I won't guess at the kitchen table."

If letters are not issued: "Then today's product is the walk, the numbers, and a list for your attorney. I will not ask you to sign a listing."

If they have no attorney: "Most estates need one. We can give you more than one name. You pick. We don't get paid for that introduction."

---

## 7. Letters, plain

**Slide 7. PG. Only if question 1 was fuzzy. Otherwise one sentence and move.**

"Letters are the court paper that names who can act for the estate. Families call them letters of authority. Until that is clear, or your attorney tells us the heirs are the ones who sign, the house stays off the market. That pause is normal. It is not us dragging our feet."

Do not explain vesting statutes, muniment of title, or creditor periods unless they ask. If they ask whether they qualify for a simpler court path: "That's your attorney's call. We can work the house around whatever timeline they give us. We can't tell you that you qualify."

---

## 8. Empty house

**Slide 8. PG. Skip if owner-occupied and staying through launch.**

"While it sits empty, someone has to own keys, utilities, the lawn, and a leak. We'll do the house side of that. You confirm the insurance. Every month it sits has a cost. That's part of why I want you to see a cash number and a market number together, not one of them."

If they brought bills: "We'll use your tax, insurance, and utility numbers. I don't carry a generic 'empty houses cost this much' figure, because it would be made up."

---

## 9. Two paths

**Slide 9. CORE. Do this before the comp slideshow, so the comps have a job.**

"Two ways. Same team. I'll show you both numbers. I'm not paid more if you take cash. I'm not the buyer."

"As-is cash means offers from independent buyers we can bring. Fewer showings. Usually a lower price. You can reject every one."

"Open market means we list it, photograph it properly, and let buyers compete. That takes the prep and the time. Commission at closing, nothing up front."

"Cash isn't always better. Neither is waiting. The empty months cost money. The discount for speed costs money. You'll see both."

If you do not have a live cash number today: "I don't have an independent cash offer in hand yet. I won't invent one. I will get you a real one by [DAY], and you already get the market range today."

---

## 10. Similar homes

**Slide 10. CORE. Real rows only.**

"These are the homes I'm using. Sold, for sale now, and under contract if we have them. I'll tell you which ones are nicer, which are worse, and which are noise."

Go through three. Not twelve. For each: what sold or what they're asking, and one sentence on condition versus this house.

"A fixed-up house is not your comp if we sell as-is. I'll say the adjustment out loud rather than hide it in a spreadsheet."

If a website estimate comes up: "Those are a starting rumor. We don't price from them."

---

## 11. The range

**Slide 11. CORE. Say the number. Do not save it for the end.**

"If we listed in this condition, I would recommend [RANGE], and I would expect [PLAIN EXPECTATION: showings in the first two weeks / a longer sit / buyers asking for repairs]. Here's why, from the three homes we just looked at."

"If we [clean out / do the short repair list], the range moves to [RANGE OR 'I need a contractor number before I pretend this is precise']. I'm not going to quote you a fantasy return on a kitchen."

Then ask: "Does that match the dollar you had in your head, or are we far apart?"

Stay quiet. If they want a higher number than the sales support, say so: "I can list it there. The first two weeks will tell us, and I'll come back with the feedback rather than cheerlead. Overpricing is how houses go stale."

**PG**

"If the family needs the higher number to feel fair, we aim the prep and the time at that. If the family needs it done, we aim at cash or a sharp as-is list. I won't pick that for the people who aren't here. I'll write the same range to all of them."

---

## 12. Condition choice

**Slide 12. CORE**

"Three prep choices. Not three sales pitches."

- "As it sits. Honest photos. Price matches the condition. Fastest to launch."
- "Clean-out and cosmetics. Family keepsakes out, then photos. Usually the right call when the house is full and the bones are fine."
- "A short repair list. Only items a buyer will price in anyway. You approve every dollar before someone starts. I don't have a program that spends your money in the background."

Do not describe funded repairs unless the written terms are in your bag and you can explain who pays if it never closes. If they ask because they saw the website: "If we do that, it will be on one written page: who pays, when, and what happens if we don't close. I won't do it from memory today."

**PG**

"Clean-out comes after keepsakes. I won't schedule a crew to decide what matters."

---

## 13. Marketing this house

**Slide 13. CORE. Point at this house, not at a brand reel.**

"Photos after prep, not before. A proofed listing in the local MLS. The sites that pick it up from there. A one-page flyer. A note to agents who actually might have a buyer. Showing rules that fit how you live, or how empty the house is. A weekly note from us, in writing."

"Video or drone only if we book it. I won't put it on the plan as decoration."

"What I will not promise: billboards, a visitor count, or a crowd we don't have."

**PG**

"If it's vacant, showings by appointment. No open house in a raw week unless you want one. Remarks stay factual. I won't write 'must sell' on an estate."

---

## 14. First 30 days

**Slide 14. CORE**

"Week one we lock the path, the price, and the signer, and you get copies. Week two is photos and you see the remarks before they publish. Week three you're live and the weekly note starts. Week four we read the showings and decide if price and condition still match."

"If the house isn't ready, or we're waiting on your attorney, we hold the launch. A sloppy first week is expensive."

Only if you have confirmed that week's Realtracs Coming Soon rules, add one sentence on the status name, whether showings are allowed, and how long. If you have not confirmed, do not say "coming soon."

---

## 15. How they will see offers

**Slide 15. CORE**

"Every offer hits one page: price, cash or loan, closing date, contingencies, what they're asking us to fix, and a net sketch with the costs we know. Unknowns stay labeled unknown."

"If two offers come in, you see them together. I recommend. You decide. I am not paid more to push the cash one."

"A loan is not a dirty word, and cash is not a trophy. Cash removes the lender. It can also be a big discount. We'll price that difference in dollars."

---

## 16. Contract to keys

**Slide 16. CORE**

"After you accept, the work is a calendar. Inspection, appraisal, the buyer's loan, title, repairs you agreed to, a final walk, then closing figures before you sign them. I'll tell you what can kill the deal while we can still do something about it."

"You get the closing numbers before the appointment, not as a surprise at the table."

**PG**

"If your attorney says closing has to wait on the court, we wait. I won't tell a buyer the judge signs on Friday. You can sign remotely in most cases. You don't have to fly in for our convenience. Keys, utilities, and any leftover contents get a last pass so the estate isn't paying a lawn crew next month."

---

## 17. How we get paid

**Slide 17. CORE. Say the rate. Do not mumble it.**

"Nothing up front. If we list, we're paid [RATE] at closing. That number is negotiable. It isn't set by law. It's on the agreement in plain sight."

"We're not paid more if you take cash. We're not the buyer. We don't pay your attorney a referral fee, and they don't pay us."

"There isn't a second invoice for 'managing the house.'"

Then stop. Let them react.

If they lead with "another agent will do it for less": "Ask them what the weekly note, the offer page, and the vacant-house work look like. Compare the work and the number. Don't compare the number alone. If you want us at a different rate, say so. We'll tell you yes or no today."

Do not cut the rate in the first thirty seconds to win the room. Do not insult the other agent.

---

## 18. Buyer's agent

**Slide 18. CORE**

"The buyer has their own agent, and that agent has a written agreement with the buyer before they tour. Some sellers offer to pay some or all of that, so more buyers will come. Some don't."

"If you want to offer it, we write the amount first. It does not go in the agent-only notes. You can also wait and decide offer by offer. I'd rather you choose with your eyes open than copy what a neighbor did in 2022."

Write down what they decided, including "not today."

---

## 19. Keepsakes, clean-out, cars

**Slide 19. PG**

"Order is: your family picks what stays, then we coordinate the estate sale or the haul-off, then we photograph. Receipts go to you for your attorney or CPA. We don't appraise the contents and we don't file the inventory."

"If there's a car, we'll line up a sale or a move once you know who holds the title. We don't set the estate's value on it."

"I meet the vendors at the house when you're out of town. You shouldn't have to fly in for a dumpster."

---

## 20. One update for the whole family

**Slide 20. PG**

"You name the list. Co-executor, heirs, attorney. Same weekly note. Same offer page. I won't give one sibling a private version."

"Once we're engaged, we can open the family portal: status, documents, a place for an heir to ask us something the others don't see, and the vendors on this file. It does not text you by itself yet. Urgent news is still a call."

"If you want your attorney copied, say so. We'll follow your lead on that."

---

## 21. Weekly rhythm

**Slide 21. CORE**

"While it's live, you get a weekly note: showings, what buyers said, anything new nearby, and one recommendation. Offers the same day. You can forward the note as-is."

"If it's quiet, we don't vanish. Week four we already planned to talk about price, condition, or time."

---

## 22. Next step

**Slide 22. CORE. Pick one ask. Say it. Then stop talking.**

**If they are ready and the signer is clear**

"The next step is the listing paperwork at [RATE], price at [PRICE], prep plan [AS-IS / CLEAN-OUT / LIGHT PREP]. I'll walk the pages in plain language. You get copies today."

Then walk the agreement. Point at compensation, length, and what cancels it. Do not rush initials.

**If they are ready but the signer is not clear (PG)**

"I won't have you sign a listing today. Next step is a note to you and your attorney with the range, the two paths, and what we can do before letters. I'll send it today. Can I copy [ATTORNEY]?"

**If they need the other person**

"I'll send the recap today in language you can forward. Let's put a call on the calendar for [DAY] after they've seen it. I'm not going to squeeze a signature out of half the decision."

**If they are not hiring you**

"Thanks for the walk. The range we discussed still stands if you want it in an email. No hard feelings and no follow-up drip."

Then leave. Shelly does not enroll them in a newsletter because the meeting was polite.

---

## Same day, before dinner

**CORE**

Send the recap email from the packet copy file. Fill every blank from the meeting. Same email to every authorized person. No "real" version and "family" version.

**PG**

If the attorney is on the list, they get that same email, not a more casual one.

Log letter status, keys, next date, and who signs, where Shelly and Branton can see it.

---

## Lines we do not say

- "The court will approve this."
- "I can get you a guaranteed number."
- "Cash is always better."
- "We're the top team in Tennessee."
- "Sign tonight so we don't lose the buyer." There is no buyer yet.
- "Our hearts go out to you" as a paragraph.
- A repair ROI you cannot show.
- Another agent's name, mocked.
- Anything about a funeral, an obituary, or "I specialize in difficult times" as an opener.

---

## If they ask a legal question

"I can't answer that as your agent. Ask [ATTORNEY], or we get you names if you don't have one. What I can do today is the house."

Then write the question in the recap so the attorney sees it. Do not research your way into an opinion in the driveway.
