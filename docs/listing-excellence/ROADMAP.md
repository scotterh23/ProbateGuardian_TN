# Seller roadmap: first contact through aftercare

Operating map for Hardesty Group and Probate Guardians. Seller-facing picture is the one-page road in the packet. This file is what the team runs.

**Tags:** CORE applies to both. PG applies to estate and probate property. Skip PG steps on a normal listing.

**Legal line, every phase:** we are licensed real estate agents. We are not attorneys or CPAs. We do not give legal advice and we do not file at court. If a step needs the attorney, the roadmap says so and stops.

## Phase 1. First contact

**Seller sees:** a person, not a funnel. A time to walk the house. Nothing to sign on the call.

**CORE**

1. Shelly or Scott answers. Listen before you describe services.
2. Write down: address, who is calling, who else owns or decides, why they are selling, timing, whether anyone lives there, whether they already have an agent.
3. Do not quote a price, a commission, or a "probably worth" number on the first call. Say we price from the house and from real sales, at the appointment.
4. Book the walk. Confirm who will be there (Scott, and Branton if the file needs two).
5. Same-day confirmation text. Short. No pitch.

**PG branch (ask on the first call, gently)**

- Are they the executor, a co-executor, an heir, or calling for someone else?
- Is there a probate attorney? Name and whether we may speak with the office. If the attorney referred them, follow the attorney's instruction on when to contact the family.
- Has the court issued letters (the paper that names who can act)? If they do not know, that is normal. Write "unknown."
- Is the house vacant? Who has keys? Are utilities on?
- Out of state or local?
- Other people who must see the numbers before a decision?
- Is there a car, or a house full of belongings, they also need help with? Note it. Do not start a clean-out on the first call.
- Grief timing. If they are in the first days after a death and only need the house secured, offer the walk and the vacant checklist. Do not push a listing date.

**Do not**

- Mail or call from an obituary.
- Say the court will approve a sale.
- Imply we are the cash buyer.
- Promise funded repairs, a timeline, or a net number.

**Exit:** appointment on the calendar, packet clock started (phase 2).

## Phase 2. Before the appointment

**Seller sees:** a PDF they can forward, then a printed copy at the house or the day before if it will really arrive. A one-line text the morning of the meeting.

**CORE (Emily)**

1. Build the packet from the Canva template. Fill name, address, date, who is attending.
2. Pull a preliminary set: 3 to 5 sold, 2 to 3 for sale, pendings if they exist. Same town or micro-area, similar size, condition noted. If the pull is thin, use the bridge box in the packet. Do not invent homes. Do not put a recommended list price in the PDF.
3. Scott scans the pull before it goes out. If he has not seen it, send the bridge page, not a half-checked price.
4. Email the PDF 24 to 48 hours before. Subject is the address and the meeting time, not a slogan. Body says they can forward it to anyone else who decides.
5. Print letter-size. Pocket folder is enough. No gift.
6. Morning-of text: look forward to the time, bring questions from anyone who cannot be there. No pitch.

**PG add**

- Cover line uses their name and the property. Decedent name only if Scott confirms the family wants it on paper. No case number.
- Include the four probate pages.
- If an attorney is in the file, copy the attorney only when the caller or the attorney asked for that.
- Bring-list on the last page: letters status, keys, utilities, co-executor contact preference.

**INTERNAL research before we roll up (not printed)**

Adapted from the public KW pre-listing checklist. Do the parts that change advice:

- Sold, active, pending, expired in the micro-area
- Tax record, owner name on record, lot
- HOA if the record shows one
- Drive-by or prior photo of the exterior if we have not been inside
- PG: letter status, signer question, vacancy, keys, utilities, attorney, who else must agree

**Exit:** they have the paper before we arrive, or we hand it to them as we walk in and say the email copy is the one to forward.

## Phase 3. Appointment

**Seller sees:** a walk, then a sit-down. Both numbers if we have them. A next step, not a trap.

Full words: [PRESENTATION-SCRIPT.md](PRESENTATION-SCRIPT.md).

**CORE order**

1. Walk the house before the deck. Notes on condition, smells, roof, HVAC, floors, what stays.
2. Ask what done looks like (date, money, hassle, who they answer to).
3. Team, one minute.
4. Two paths. Open market and as-is cash. Say we are not the buyer and not paid more for cash.
5. Show the real homes. Then say the range and why. Tie it to condition.
6. Prep choice: sell as it sits, clean out and cosmetics, or a short repair list. No invented return-on-repair percentage.
7. Marketing for this house. First 30 days.
8. How offers will be shown. How we are paid. Buyer-agent pay in plain words.
9. Next step. Same-day written recap either way.

**PG fluency block, before the path recommendation**

Ask, do not quiz. If they do not know, write it down.

1. Who does the attorney say can sign? Letters issued, pending, or not started?
2. Co-executor or other heirs who must agree? How do they want to receive the same page?
3. Vacant or occupied? Insurance: have they told their insurer the house is empty? We do not advise. We tell them to ask the insurer.
4. Keys, utilities, known leaks, lawn, winter.
5. Keepsakes: has the family walked through once for what they want to keep?
6. Cars or other titled property on site?
7. Anyone living there, including a tenant? If yes, showings wait on Scott and the attorney. Do not promise a lockbox over a tenant.
8. Mortgage, HOA, or bills they already know about. Title will confirm. We do not clear title at the kitchen table.
9. What would feel fair to the person who is not in the room?

**Authority gate (say it out loud)**

We can secure, photograph for the family, and prepare numbers before letters. We do not list the house until the attorney confirms who signs. Court timing can pause a launch. We will not invent a court date.

**Exit:** they know the range, the two paths, and the single next step. Recap email the same day.

## Phase 4. Decision and authority

**Seller sees:** a one-page recap. Listing paperwork only when they are ready and the right person can sign.

**CORE**

1. Same-day recap: what we saw, the range, both paths if discussed, prep choice, what we still need, the next date.
2. If they want to hire us, walk the listing paperwork in plain language. Duration, what we will do, what ends it, our compensation, and any amount they choose to offer a buyer's agent. Compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. Say that. Do not hide the rate.
3. Give them signed copies. Do not start MLS until the agreement is actually signed and the broker's file is complete.
4. If they need a night, or a spouse, stop. The recap is the close. A follow-up call is scheduled before we leave.

**PG branch**

- Signer matches the attorney's instruction. If letters are not issued, the recap says "house work can start; listing waits on your attorney."
- Recap goes to every authorized person in the same words. No side version for one sibling.
- Attorney gets the recap when the executor wants them copied.
- No referral fee to the law firm. Say it if the attorney is new to us.
- Remote signing is fine. They do not have to fly in.
- If heirs disagree, we do not pick a side. We re-send the same numbers and wait. We are not mediators.

**Exit:** either a signed listing with a clear signer, or a dated recap and a follow-up. Both are clean exits. A muddy "we're kind of listed" is not.

## Phase 5. Prep (before the public launch)

**Seller sees:** the house getting safe and photographable. A dated photo set. A vendor schedule. No surprise invoices from us for "house management."

**CORE**

1. Seller disclosure, completed with them. We do not guess answers.
2. What stays and what goes, in writing (fixtures, appliances).
3. Lockbox and showing windows once they are ready for strangers. Extra key.
4. Utilities, HOA contact, survey or floor plan if they have one.
5. Repairs they chose, with their own vendors or vendors we introduce. They approve cost before work. We coordinate. We do not mark up a secret bill.
6. Professional photos after the house is as ready as the path requires. Virtual staging only if labeled and paired with the real photo, per MLS rules. Prefer a real clean-out over digital furniture on an estate house.
7. Pre-list title order through the closing company when Scott's practice is to do that early. The point, from working estate agents in the RISMedia piece, is to find liens before we are under contract. We do not interpret title. We introduce the problem to the attorney or closer while there is still time.

**PG branch: secure the vacant house**

Order that respects the family:

1. Keys and access list. Who may enter. Locksmith if the attorney or executor asks.
2. Utilities on or winterized, their accounts, not ours, unless a written arrangement says otherwise.
3. Lawn and a simple security look (lights, locks, obvious leaks). Branton or Scott on site.
4. Tell them, in writing, to confirm vacant-house insurance with their insurer. We are not insurance agents.
5. **Keepsakes first.** Family walk, in person or by video. Photo inventory of what will ship. Then estate sale or haul-off and donation. We coordinate crews. We are not the estate-sale company. Donation receipts go to the executor for the attorney or CPA.
6. **Cars and personal property.** After keepsakes, we can line up a sale path for vehicles and loose property (who has the title, where it sits, which local help). We do not appraise for the estate inventory and we do not file the inventory. If a car is in a garage that blocks photos, it gets scheduled before photo day.
7. Photo day only after the family says the keepsake pass is done, or they explicitly tell us to photograph around remaining items.
8. Carrying cost conversation stays conceptual unless we are looking at their actual tax, insurance, and utility figures. Do not print a generic "empty houses cost $X."

**Portal (PG, when engaged)**

Invite executor, heirs they name, and attorney. Current portal: status, timeline, documents, private questions to us, vendor list we curate. Do not promise text alerts. The product does not send them yet.

**Exit:** house matches the path (as-is honest, or cleaned), photos in hand, signer still valid, attorney not surprised.

## Phase 6. Listing file and go-live

**Seller sees:** "It is live" only when it is actually live. A link. Showing rules.

**CORE**

1. MLS complete and proofed: price, photos, remarks, map, directions. Primary photo is the house, not a logo.
2. No offer of compensation written into public or private MLS remarks. Realtracs forbids it.
3. Syndication follows the MLS. Do not promise a specific site count.
4. Showing instructions match real life (dogs, tenants, vacant, appointment-only).
5. Sign, if they want one and the neighborhood allows it. Some estates will not want a sign the first week. Ask.
6. Flyer for the file and for agents. One page.
7. Tell the agents we actually know. No fake "database of millions."
8. Status changes on the MLS clock (generally 48 hours). Shelly keeps the checklist. Scott is responsible for accuracy.

**Launch sequence**

- Default: prep, photos, then active.
- Coming Soon/Hold only if a signed listing exists, the seller instructs it in writing, and Scott has confirmed that week's Realtracs rules (showings or not, how long, whether it syndicates). The July 28, 2026 rule text and the older help-page text do not match. See the competitive brief. Until confirmed, do not advertise a 30-day no-showing product in print.

**PG branch**

- Do not go active to "hold a date" if the attorney has not cleared the signer.
- Showing rules for a vacant estate: appointment, lights and locks checked, no open house during a raw week unless the executor asks for one.
- Copy the attorney the live link if they are on the update list.
- Remarks stay factual. Do not write "estate sale" or "must sell" or "court ordered" unless the attorney says that phrase is accurate and useful. Desperate copy costs money.

**Exit:** listing is accurate, seller has the link, showing rules are in the lockbox notes.

## Phase 7. Marketing while it is live

**Seller sees:** a weekly written note. Same-day news if something matters (a serious showing pattern, a price conversation, an offer).

**CORE weekly note**

- How many showings, in plain numbers
- What buyers' agents actually said, themes not gossip
- What else went under contract or listed nearby that changes our advice
- One recommendation: hold, adjust prep, or talk price
- What we will do next week

**CORE activity (only what we will really do)**

- Feedback requests after showings
- Price checked against new sales, not against our feelings
- Agent follow-up on the buyers who asked real questions
- Social or email only if the account and the copy are real
- Video or drone only if booked in phase 5

**If it is quiet**

Use the SERHANT. guide's adult version: the plan for a slow house is decided in advance. Options we will say out loud: price, condition, audience, or pause. We do not disappear and then surprise them with a reduction.

**PG branch**

- Weekly note to the full authorized list, same attachment
- Heir questions go to us. We do not referee sibling texts. Portal questions stay private to the sender, which is how the portal works
- Vacant check on a rhythm Branton and the executor agree (for example a weekly drive-by). Write what we saw
- Carrying cost gets mentioned when the house sits, as a reason to look at both paths again, not as a scare tactic

**Exit:** seller can tell a spouse or a sibling what happened this week without calling us.

## Phase 8. Offers

**Seller sees:** every offer on one comparison page. Net to them after the items we know. Strengths and weak spots in normal words.

**CORE, for each offer**

- Price
- Cash or loan, and whether a lender letter exists. We call the lender when there is a loan. We do not treat "cash" as automatically better
- Closing date
- Inspection, appraisal, sale of the buyer's house, or other contingencies
- What they asked us to fix or pay
- Earnest money
- A net sketch using the commission and closing costs we already disclosed. Unknown title bills are marked unknown, not guessed

**How we present**

- More than one offer: same grid, same hour, to the seller. No favorite hidden in a phone call
- We recommend. They decide
- Counter, accept, or decline goes out in writing the same day they decide
- Backup offers: explain them. MLS status follows their choice (still showing, or not)

**PG branch**

- Same grid to every authorized person. If one heir calls for "the real story," they get the same page
- Attorney sees the accepted terms when the executor wants a court or file copy
- Do not accept on the porch if the signer is wrong. Pause and call the attorney
- A cash investor offer and a retail offer sit on the same grid. We are not paid more if they take the cash offer. Say that again on the grid so nobody has to wonder

**Exit:** signed response, status updated, everyone on the list has the same paper.

## Phase 9. Under contract

**Seller sees:** a calendar in plain language. What the buyer must do. What we need from them. What can kill the deal.

**CORE beats**

1. MLS status within the required window. Showing or not showing, their choice, written.
2. Earnest money tracked with the closing company. We do not invent escrow habits that conflict with the broker's trust rules. Follow the broker.
3. Inspection scheduled. We attend or have Branton there on a vacant house. Review the report with the seller. Negotiate repairs without panic. Get their approval before anyone spends.
4. Appraisal. Share the comps we used if the appraiser asks through the proper channel. If it comes in low, explain the choices: price, buyer brings cash, or the deal ends. No promises.
5. Loan. Weekly check with the lender. Tell the seller when it is approved and when it is stuck.
6. Title. Issues go to the closer and, on PG files, the attorney. We do not "solve" a clouded title ourselves.
7. Repair receipts and re-checks if we agreed to repairs.
8. Final walkthrough scheduled. Vacant houses: we walk the day before so a leak is ours to report, not a closing-table surprise.

**PG branch**

- Court date or creditor timing: the attorney tells us if closing must wait. We schedule vendors and the buyer around that. We do not tell the buyer "the judge will sign Friday."
- Remote seller: shipping a deed, or e-sign, through the closing company. They do not have to fly unless the closer or the court requires a body in the room.
- Occupancy: estate houses are usually empty at close. If a relative is still there, that is a contract problem to solve in writing, early.
- Personal property left behind: contract says what conveys. Leftover contents are a clean-out, not a gift to the buyer, unless the contract says so.

**Exit:** clear to close, or a written problem with an owner and a date.

## Phase 10. Close

**Seller sees:** figures before they sign, a net that matches the last sheet within explained changes, keys and utilities handed off.

**CORE**

1. Closing figures reviewed before the appointment. Explain changes from the offer net sheet.
2. Tell them what to bring (ID, and whatever the closer asked).
3. Keys, garage openers, mailbox, codes. HOA or gate letters if any.
4. Utilities transfer date. On a vacant house, do not cancel heat before the deed if a freeze is possible. Coordinate the shutoff day with the buyer side.
5. Forward mail is their job or the attorney's. We can remind. We do not file a change of address for an estate as if we were the executor.
6. MLS closed on the broker's clock.
7. Same-day text: it recorded, here is what we know. The formal closing statement comes from the closer.

**PG branch**

- Attorney or executor confirms funds instructions. We do not tell them which heir to pay.
- Portal status to Closed. Documents they are allowed to hold (closing disclosure copy if the closer releases it) go in the vault.
- Final vacant checklist: thermostat, water, lockbox off, sign down.

**Exit:** recorded deed, seller knows the number, house is not our silent responsibility anymore.

## Phase 11. Aftercare

**Seller sees:** one useful note, then a human check-in. Not a drip campaign.

**CORE**

- Thank-you the way Scott actually talks. No poem.
- One check-in a week or two later: did the closer, the HOA, or the warranty (if there was one) leave a loose end?
- Ask for a review only if the experience was real and they offer warmth. Never draft a fake quote. If they write one and approve it, it can enter the proof file Emily keeps.
- Refer them to a destination agent only if they are actually moving and we know a real person. No random referral mill.

**PG branch**

- Clean-out vendor and lawn vendor get an end date in writing so the estate is not billed next month.
- Cars or leftover personal property: either done, or a named owner and a date. Do not leave a boat in the driveway as an implied promise.
- Portal stays up for the executor and attorney for whatever period Scott and the attorney agree, then access is closed on purpose. Say that date.
- Heir questions after close about "who got what" go to the executor and the attorney. We answer house questions only.
- If they want a sentence we can use later, ask once, plainly.

**Exit:** file closed, vendors stopped, proof file updated only with real paper.

## Probate overlay, one page

Use this when a normal checklist is not enough.

| Moment | House can proceed | House waits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First call | Listen, book the walk | Do not price or list |
| Before letters | Secure, family photos, numbers, clean-out plan | MLS, sign, public marketing, accepting an offer |
| Letters issued, attorney says the signer is clear | Listing paperwork, prep, launch | Still wait if the attorney says the court must approve terms |
| Heirs disagree | Same numbers to everyone | Do not pick a path for them |
| Keepsakes not chosen | Secure and wait on photo day | Do not estate-sale the house out from under the family |
| Under contract, court date unknown | Inspections can often be scheduled | Do not promise the buyer's agent a closing day the attorney has not blessed |
| Close | Keys, utilities, portal, vendor stop | Distribution of money is the estate's job |
