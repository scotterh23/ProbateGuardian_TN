# Competitive brief: packets, presentations, and seller roadmaps

Prepared September 2026 for Probate Guardians TN and Hardesty Group.

**Question this file answers:** What do strong listing agents and listing systems actually put in front of a seller before and during the listing appointment, and what roadmap do they walk? Which of those patterns transfer to a Middle Tennessee executor, and which transfer to a normal Hardesty listing?

**Rules used while writing**

- Named people and firms below are real. Claims are tied to a public page, PDF, or article.
- Short quotations are from those sources. Nothing in quotation marks was written for this file.
- If a habit is widely repeated by coaches but not documented on a named producer's own site, it is marked **Hypothesis**.
- Compass, Ashton, SERHANT., Jills Zeder, Fridrich & Clark, and HomesUSA publish their own results and audience numbers. Those numbers are **their marketing or their interviews**, not numbers we can put on our packet.
- No Probate Guardians or Hardesty production statistic is stated here, because this research did not audit our closed files.

**How to use it:** steal the pattern, skip the costume. Carrie does not design a SERHANT. studio. Scott does not promise an Ashton billboard. The packet answers the checklist an executor is now told to demand.

## 1. What "on par" means for this team

High-volume and high-end listing machines win appointments with four moves that show up across very different brands:

1. **A pre-read before the meeting**, personalized to the address, so price and process are not a cold open.
2. **A specific marketing plan** (assets, sequence, who does what), not a list of adjectives.
3. **Price said plainly**, from sold and competing homes, with more than one scenario.
4. **A roadmap of the whole sale**, including what happens after the offer, so the seller is not afraid of the next step.

Luxury teams add production value (photo, film, brochure, press). Volume teams add a visible operating checklist and a comparison of ways to sell. Executor-facing guides add a written proposal the fiduciary can file: who does the work, what is excluded, how people are updated, and how everyone is paid.

Probate Guardians already has the right promise in locked language: take the house off the desk, stay in the attorney's lane, show both numbers, get paid at close, stay a small team. The gap versus the best listing agents is not a new slogan. It is **system**: a leave-behind an executor can forward, a presentation that puts price in the middle instead of the end, and a roadmap the team can run on Tuesday and on a normal Hardesty listing the same week.

The rejected Lake Chateau 2-pager (September 2026 draft) had the right lines and the wrong weight. It read as a flyer. The best packets read as a short working proposal. Target remains a kitchen-table length: 12 pages for a normal listing, 16 when the probate insert is in. A 30-page binder is a sales novel. That length judgment is carried forward from the September 2026 internal research brief, which itself cited several agent-education articles. Treat the page count as a **design decision we are adopting**, not as a law of selling.

## 3. Named agents, teams, and systems

### 3.1 Keller Williams public listing system

**Who:** Not one human producer. The most widely copied U.S. listing **system**. Two public artifacts do the teaching: a pre-listing packet PDF hosted on kw.com image servers, and a checklist PDF titled in the file name "What I Do For You."

**Sources**

- Pre-listing packet PDF: https://images.kw.com/docs/0/7/6/076653/1199869332180_Pre_Listing_Packet.pdf
- Checklist PDF: https://images.kw.com/docs/3/6/3/363293/1440958319265_What_I_Do_For_You.pdf
- A later KW-affiliated office answer sheet (December 26, 2024) describes listing as two meetings and adds seller concessions and buyer-broker compensation after the 2024 practice changes: https://myofficeanswerpro.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Listing-Appointment-Seller-and-Listing-Agent-122624.pdf

**What they put in the packet.** The hosted packet is a template: a "Dear [name]" letter, a customer-service commitment, and "Ten Steps to Selling Your Home" (prep, price, paperwork, marketing, showings, offer, negotiate, inspections and appraisal, closing prep, close). The letter claims the programs outlined give "the greatest possibility of selling your home for the highest price in the shortest period of time with the least amount of hassles." That sentence is their template voice. It is too boastful for our cover note. Steal the **shape** (note, commitment, ten steps). Skip the superlative.

**What they do in the appointment.** The checklist splits work into pre-listing research (items 1 to 20: confirm the appointment, pull competing listings, 18 months of sales, days on market, tax roll, CMA, ownership and deed, lot, zoning, legal names, curb appeal), then the presentation (items 21 to 33: market overview, credentials, CMA including sold, current, and expired, pricing strategy, MLS and web marketing, agency choices, review the listing contract and sign). After the signature, the list runs measurements, loans, HOA, utilities, a **vacancy checklist** (item 63), disclosures, photos, lockbox, weekly market study, **weekly update calls** (item 108), a **net sheet on each offer** (item 111), counseling the seller on strengths and weaknesses of each offer (item 112), then inspection, appraisal, closing figures, and a thin after-close follow-up.

The December 2024 office sheet updates the choreography: meeting one is the walk, motivation, improvements, loan balance. Meeting two is marketing, compensation including possible buyer concessions, CMA, seller net sheet, then forms. It tells the agent to "Discuss potential Seller's Broker compensation and potential Buyer concessions (which may include Buyer's Broker compensation)."

**Roadmap.** Their roadmap is the ten steps plus the post-signature checklist. It is house-sale generic. It has no probate, no co-executor, no letters.

**Steal:** research before you arrive; sold + active + expired; price in the meeting; vacancy checklist; weekly update; one net picture per offer; two-step meeting (walk, then sit); say buyer-agent pay out loud.

**Skip:** the 180-item list as a customer handout; template bragging; any implication that our packet is a KW product.

**Hypothesis:** many Nashville agents still use a descendant of this checklist because KW training standardized it. We did not audit local brokers to prove that.

### 3.2 SERHANT. and Ryan Serhant

**Who:** Ryan Serhant, founder of SERHANT., New York, national media profile, luxury and high-production branding.

**Sources**

- Seller's Guide PDF: https://go.ryanserhant.com/hubfs/SERHANT.%20Files/Sellers-Guide.pdf
- Inman, March 5, 2019, "How To Sell Like Ryan Serhant": https://www.inman.com/2019/03/05/how-to-sell-like-ryan-serhant/
- Realtor.com interview (pricing and presentation): https://www.realtor.com/news/reality-tv/exclusive-ryan-serhant-reveals-the-reason-why-right-now-is-a-fantastic-time-to-sell-a-house/
- SERHANT. blog, March 2, 2026, "Launching Strong": https://serhant.com/blog/launching-strong-how-smart-pricing-and-marketing-set-the-tone-for-a-successful-sale
- Inman, April 29, 2022, on turning one listing into many content pieces: https://www.inman.com/2022/04/29/9-pieces-of-content-from-1-listing-ryan-serhant-is-ready-to-teach-you-how/

**Packet / guide.** The Seller's Guide is a consumer roadmap, not a CMA. Table of contents: why you are selling; looking for an agent (questions to ask yourself and the agent); how to review a listing agreement; prep (declutter, clean, repair, paint, stage, virtual stage as a cheaper option); market (professional photos, open-house and showing schedule, where it will be listed, traditional media, social, the house's story); offers (under asking, at and over, cash vs financing); if it is not selling (price, timing, re-stage, or wait); contract, appraisal, closing; people of the process (agents, stagers, photographers, lawyers, appraisers). It says agent compensation "is based entirely on commission."

The back of the guide sells SERHANT. ADX, Studios, and ID Lab, plus "100M+ impressions per month," "3.6M+ followers," and "75M+ Youtube views." That is their amplification story.

**Presentation habits (interviews, not a published slide list).** Inman 2019 summarized his advice as: explain the strategy, including price, from day one; laying out the whole process takes fear out; quality of service over quantity of business. Realtor.com quoted him: "With sellers, the biggest mistake right now is they don't pay attention to price and presentation." The March 2026 company blog says professional photography, video, floor plans, and thoughtful copy "are no longer differentiators. They are expectations," and that pricing, staging, creative assets, and distribution should be aligned before the home goes live.

**Skip inside an otherwise useful guide:** the guide says an all-cash offer means the seller does not have to worry about a mortgage denial and therefore "all-cash offers are almost always better." Do not teach that. Our sellers, especially estates, need both numbers because a discount for speed can be larger than the carrying cost, or smaller. The agent shows the math. The agent does not declare a winner in the leave-behind.

**Steal:** why-you-are-selling as the first real question; questions the seller should ask us (we should answer them before they ask); people of the process; "if it is not selling" as a planned conversation, not a panic; coordinated launch; honest price early.

**Skip:** impression counts, in-house studio claims, content-machine advice as a client promise, cash-always-wins.

### 3.4 Compass three-phase marketing

**Who:** Compass, the brokerage, not a single agent. Included because their coming-soon system is the clearest public version of "do not stumble onto the open market half ready."

**Source:** https://www.compass.com/coming-soon/

**What they publish.** Three phases: Private Exclusive (they say early exposure to a large agent network without days on market), Coming Soon (Compass.com and Redfin, which they describe as protection from days-on-market and price-drop history), then public websites. They tell the seller the options are in a disclosure form. They publish their own outcome association for a 12-month period ending March 21, 2026: "4.6% Higher Closing Price," "34% Faster to Contract," "29% Fewer Public Price Drops." Two client quotations on that page are Compass clients in Missouri and Colorado. Do not reuse them.

**Steal:** the idea of a **sequence**. Prep and photos first. Go live when the house matches the price. Explain why a sloppy first week is expensive.

**Skip:** naming Compass phases, quoting their percentages, or implying we have a private network of hundreds of thousands of agents.

**Local version, with a conflict we must not paper over.** Realtracs (Middle Tennessee MLS) has a Coming Soon/Hold status. In the rules PDF fetched for this brief, the header says **Last Revised: July 28, 2026**:

> Coming Soon/Hold: An exclusive listing agreement has been executed, but the seller(s) instructs the Participant to test pricing and marketing options. Days on market do not accrue and changes to the list price are not recorded while a listing is in the Coming Soon/Hold status.

Source: https://supporthub.realtracs.com/hubfs/MLS%20Rules,%20User%20Agreement,%20and%20Participation%20Agreements/Realtracs%20Rules%2006182025.pdf (file header date is July 28, 2026 even though the URL contains an earlier date).

An older support-hub definition page still says Coming Soon/Hold means the seller has instructed in writing that the property cannot be shown, that showings including virtual showings are prohibited, that days on market do not accumulate, and that the status can last up to 30 days with an available-for-showing date. It also says the status can syndicate outward unless the agent opts out, and that yard signs, social, and email can still run. Source: https://supporthub.realtracs.com/definitions-listing-status

**Those two public texts do not match.** Carrie must not print "no showings for 30 days" as a promise. Scott confirms the live rule in the broker's Realtracs account before the first 30-days page treats Coming Soon as a product. Until then the packet says we can hold the public launch until photos and prep match, and we will tell you which MLS status that uses.

**Sources**

- Guaranteed Offer terms: https://www.garyashton.com/terms-and-conditions
- Jeff Checko bio page (relocation director; auction described there): https://www.garyashton.com/team/jeff-checko-licensed-real-estate-agent

**What they put in front of sellers.** The homepage is a menu of programs: Guaranteed Cash Offer, Ashton Auction Advantage, "Don't Sell w/o the Intel," and Guaranteed Close in 14 Days. The table headers include no showing, no open houses, pick your closing date, repair required, remove sale-of-home contingency, use with any home, multiple buyer offers, maximize financial return. This research did not rely on guessing which boxes are checked. The **table itself** is the lesson: one page, several ways to sell, benefits in rows a seller can point at.

Their terms page says the guaranteed offer is through third-party investors, requires a separate listing contract, and that investors may or may not offer. The Checko page describes the auction, with Halo Realty, as a set sale date, no regular showings or open houses, and an optional public preview day. Marketing claims on the Checko page and the luxury blog include radio, billboards, TopGolf, Titans, Predators, and "well over 10,000 visitors a day" plus "more than 100 inquiries a day." The expired-listing post claims professional photo and video, paid platforms, and daily tracking. Those are their claims.

**Steal:** one comparison page for two real paths, in the seller's language (showings or not, who picks the date, repairs or not, one buyer or the market). Daily or at least weekly tracking, stated as a habit we will keep (weekly written, plus same-day on offers).

**Skip:** their program names, 14-day guarantee, auction, stadium creative, visitor counts, and any sentence that sounds like we purchase the house. Our cash path is independent buyers, both numbers, we are not the buyer, we are not paid more if they take cash.

### 3.8 Ben Caballero and HomesUSA

**Who:** Dallas. RealTrends has ranked him the top individual U.S. agent by transaction count for years because HomesUSA lists new homes for production builders. Included so we do not confuse "most transactions" with "best listing appointment."

**Sources**

- HomesUSA home: https://homesusa.com/

**What he actually does.** HousingWire reported 6,409 transaction sides in 2020 and $2.2 billion in volume, and quoted the model: clients are builders, not families. ### 3.9 Seeking Agents: the executor proposal checklist

**Who:** Seeking Agents is a Phoenix comparison platform, not a listing team. Jim Gruler is identified on the page as an Arizona broker and co-founder. This is the clearest public statement of **what an executor is told to require** in a written agent proposal. It belongs in this brief because our PG packet should be that proposal.

**Source:** https://www.seekingagents.com/guides/probate-real-estate-guide/should-executors-get-multiple-agent-proposals (page says updated July 2026).

**What they tell executors to demand**

| Section | They say to ask for |
| --- | --- |
| Agent and brokerage | License, brokerage, local work, probate experience, team roles, conflicts |
| Property assessment | Condition, access, occupancy, likely buyers, what is still unknown |
| Pricing | Comparables, adjustments, range, launch price, when you will review it |
| Preparation | As-is option and any clean-out or repair plan, time, vendors, assumptions |
| Marketing | Photos, exposure, buyer targeting, showings, reporting |
| Communication | Primary contacts, update frequency, response, offer summaries, attorney coordination |
| Transaction support | Inspections, appraisal, title, deadlines, closing, and what is excluded |
| Compensation and terms | Negotiated pay, other charges, what is included, how long, how it ends |

They warn against guaranteed price, timeline, savings, court outcome, or legal-authority claims; a high price with no comparable evidence; vague services and fees; pressure to sign before authority and conflicts are clear; and any claim that the agent replaces the attorney, appraiser, inspector, contractor, or tax professional.

They also note, in the same guide's NAR settlement section, that commissions are negotiated, buyer agreements are signed before showings, and buyer-agent pay is not an automatic MLS field.

**Steal:** build the packet so a co-executor can score us on that grid. Put exclusions on the page (we are not the attorney, not the insurer, not the buyer, not the appraiser). Document the choice. Do not rank ourselves by the highest suggested price.

**Skip:** sending executors to a comparison marketplace, or writing "interview three agents" into our own cover note. That is their business. Our job is to be the proposal that survives the comparison.

## 4. Rules and law the roadmap has to respect

This section is for Scott, Branton, Emily, and Carrie so the packet does not overclaim. It is not copy.

### 4.1 Realtracs compensation rules

Same July 28, 2026 rules PDF as above.

- Section 2.9(b): communicating offers of compensation in listing content is prohibited.
- Section 3.4: participants must disclose to sellers and buyers that broker compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable, in the listing agreement and related disclosures. Any payment the listing side will make to a buyer broker or other buyer representative requires the seller's written authority **in advance**, including the amount or rate.
- Section 3.5: a written buyer agreement is required before touring, with compensation that is ascertainable, not open-ended.
- Section 2.17: status changes generally within 48 hours; binding contract status within 48 hours; closed within 72 hours.
- Section 2.19: virtual staging is allowed only with disclosure and a true "before" image next to it. Do not promise secret digital furniture as if the house were staged.

Plain-language consequence for our pay page: we agree our listing commission in writing; nobody else sets it; if the seller wants to offer something toward the buyer's agent, that number is written before it is offered; it does not go in the MLS remarks.

### 4.2 Tennessee letters and the house

Education only. The attorney decides. Customer copy stays at "your attorney tells us who can sign."

- Tennessee Code § 30-1-101: no person shall presume to enter on the administration of a deceased person's estate until they have letters testamentary or letters of administration. https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-30/chapter-1/part-1/section-30-1-101/
- Tennessee Code § 31-2-103: real property of an intestate decedent vests at death in the heirs. Real property of a testate decedent vests at death in the will beneficiaries **unless the will says the real property is administered as part of the estate under the personal representative**. Personal property vests in the personal representative once they qualify, for expenses and debts. If personal property is not enough, the personal representative may use real property under the sale-of-land statutes. https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-31/chapter-2/section-31-2-103/
- Tennessee Code § 30-2-418: in general terms, if the will does not give a power to sell real estate and the personal estate is not enough to pay debts, expenses, and taxes, the court directs a sale, with notice and a hearing. https://law.justia.com/codes/tennessee/title-30/chapter-2/part-4/section-30-2-418/

**Practical gate, not a legal opinion:** we can talk, walk, photograph for the family's own use, and prepare numbers before letters exist. We do not put the house in MLS, and we do not treat a family member as the signer, until the attorney confirms who has authority to sign the listing and the deed. Sometimes that is the personal representative. Sometimes the heirs or devisees must sign. Sometimes the court is in the loop. We do not sort that. We write down the attorney's answer.

A Tennessee Bar Association law-blog article, "Probate Potholes," walks the same vesting problem for lawyers (real estate often vests in heirs, with a court path to pull it back to pay debts). Useful as a reminder that this is a known trap, not a script. https://www.tba.org/?blAction=showEntry&blogEntry=51156&pg=LawBlog

Muniment of title appears on our public roadmap as something families can **ask the attorney** about. It stays that way. We do not determine eligibility in a listing packet.

### 4.3 What our own product already is

So the roadmap does not invent a portal.

Estate portal phase 1 (repo `estate-portal/README.md`): email login; admin invites for executor, heir, and attorney; estate status; house timeline; document vault; heirs can ask the team questions that other heirs do not see; curated vendors. **Not in phase 1:** notifications, a vendor marketplace, CRM sync, payments.

House status labels already in product code: Letters issued, Valuation, Listed, Under contract, Closed. A separate probate progress track (inventory, notice to creditors, creditor period, debts and taxes, final accounting, estate closed) is attorney-side context. We display it. We do not file it.

Public pages already promise, in substance: nothing up front; listing commission at closing; both nets in writing; not a law firm; no referral fees; clean-out coordination without being the estate-sale company; remote signing; five-business-day photo-and-numbers rhythm on attorney referrals. Sources in the repo: `probate-landing/out-of-state-executors/index.html`, `probate-landing/for-attorneys/index.html`. The packet should not contradict those lines.
